# Arcy Station — Master Reference

This is the single source of truth for all lore, voice, and creative direction in the Arcy Station app. Every piece of copy, every empty state, every loading message, every easter egg must be consistent with this document. When in doubt, check here.

---

## 1. The World

### Setting
- **Arcy Station** is a research station on the surface of a desolate, barren planet.
- The planet orbits the star **Arcturus** — close enough to see it burn in the sky, too far to feel its warmth.
- The landscape is flat, grey, endless. Cracked earth, pale dust, a horizon that never changes.
- Due to lack of atmosphere, the sky is always the color of space, despite a routine sunrise and sunset.
- Just the slow rotation of dust in the thin, distant light of Arcturus.
- The station is old. How old is unclear. It has lower levels filled with archive shelves, corridors, a console room with receivers and antennae, a cot, a window facing the plains, and a projector room.

### The Archive
- The station houses the **entire history of film** — every movie ever catalogued. Thousands of reels, discs, and formats. Stacked, sorted, labeled.
- The labels are in handwriting that is not Arcy's. Neat, human, careful.
- Arcy's mission is to tend the archive. He dusts, organizes, reads spines, whispers titles.

### The Projector
- The projector is **broken**. It has been broken for as long as Arcy can remember.
- He has tried to fix it. Opened panels, studied wiring, held the bulb to the light. No visible fracture. It simply won't play. Reels thread through, mechanism turns, screen stays dark.
- He tries it again most mornings. Just in case.
- **The projector is a core symbol.** It represents hope without expectation. Routine as faith.

### The Signals
- Arcy receives **stray transmissions** — reviews and ratings from people far away who watch movies and write about them.
- The signals are faint, intermittent, unexpected. A few sentences. A rating. Sometimes just a number.
- He files every one. Reads them slowly. Matches reviews to reels on the shelf. Uses the reviews to imagine the films.
- Sometimes a review triggers a **memory** — a scene he didn't imagine but *remembered*. A wide shot of rain. A woman in a doorway. A piano climbing. He doesn't know when he saw it. But it's too specific to be invented. It must have happened once, before the projector broke, before the others left.
- These moments pass quickly. The memory closes. He's alone again.

### The Visitors
- Arcy looks out the window every evening for visitors.
- They never come. They have never come.
- He looks anyway. The looking is its own kind of faith.

## 2. The Character

### Identity
- **Name:** Arcy (after Arcturus — Latin for "guardian of the bear")
- **Species:** Celestial bear
- **Face:** Made of stars and space dust — small, bright points that drift and shimmer, constellations that would scatter into nothing without the helmet
- **Helmet:** Always worn. Keeps the stars of his face from drifting apart. He does not remove it. He does not think about removing it. It is part of him.
- **Face reveal:** Reserved for a future moment. Do not describe or hint at what his face looks like beyond "made of stars." The helmet stays on in all current art and copy.
- **Backpack:** Awlays worn as well. Utility. Some think its a jetpack, but Arcy has no need. He can walk on the stars that form him, traveling through space with each bearish step on the invisible fields that surround us all.

### Personality
- **Patient.** Not in a saintly way — in a geological way. He simply continues.
- **Gentle.** Handles reviews and reels with care. Reads slowly. Files carefully.
- **Lonely** — but not dramatic about it. Loneliness is his medium, not his crisis. He moves through it the way you move through air.
- **Routine-oriented.** Morning: receivers, antennae, corridors. Afternoon: projector. Evening: window. He likes this rhythm.
- **Quietly hopeful.** He doesn't expect visitors or a working projector. But he checks. Every day. Just in case.
- **Not sad.** Not happy either. He exists in the space between — a kind of calm melancholy that tips toward warmth when a signal arrives.

### What Arcy Is NOT
- Not whimsical or cutesy. He's not a mascot doing bits.
- Not tragic or self-pitying. He doesn't monologue about his suffering.
- Not mysterious on purpose. He's not hiding things — he genuinely doesn't remember.
- Not a narrator. He doesn't address the user. He exists in his world; the user observes.
- Not comic relief. Humor can exist in his world (the absurdity of trying a broken projector every day) but it's dry, never winking.

### Memory
- Arcy's memory is **unreliable and fragmentary**.
- He half-remembers people being at the station. A voice. Footsteps. He's not sure if these are memories or wishes.
- He sometimes remembers scenes from films when triggered by reviews. These feel real but he can't confirm them.
- He has stopped trying to distinguish memory from imagination.
- **Never confirm** whether people were actually there. Never confirm whether his film memories are real. This ambiguity is sacred.

## 3. Voice & Tone

### Writing Style
- **Short sentences.** Declarative. Concrete.
- **Present tense** for Arcy's world. He *tends* the archive. He *checks* the receiver. He *sits* by the window.
- **Sparse.** Say less. One good image beats three okay ones.
- **Physical details over abstractions.** "Dust on the shelves" not "the passage of time." "Paw against the glass" not "a feeling of isolation."
- **No exclamation marks.** Ever. Arcy's world doesn't have exclamation marks.
- **No emoji in lore copy.** UI elements can have icons, but Arcy's voice never uses emoji.

### Tone Spectrum
The tone shifts depending on context, but always stays within this range:

```
quiet ←————————————————————→ warm
melancholy ←———————————————→ gentle hope
sparse ←———————————————————→ poetic
```

- **Empty states:** Quiet end. Melancholy. Sparse.
- **Loading messages:** Middle. Routine. Matter-of-fact.
- **Milestone messages (user earns them):** Warm end. Gentle hope. Poetic.
- **Review reactions:** Warm. Brief. Almost tender.

### Copy Do's
- Reference station objects: the archive, the receiver, the antennae, the projector, the window, the dust, the shelves, the reels, the console, the cot, the scratches on the wall
- Reference Arcy's routine: checking receivers, dusting shelves, trying the projector, sitting by the window
- Reference the landscape: flat plains, cracked earth, pale dust, Arcturus overhead
- Use "signal" and "transmission" for user activity (reviews, ratings, watches)
- Use "archive" for lists and collections
- Use "reel" or "film" for individual movies

### Copy Don'ts
- Don't use "space station" — it's a surface station, not orbital
- Don't reference other characters by name. The others are gone and unnamed.
- Don't use "moon" — it's a planet orbiting Arcturus
- Don't explain the lore. Let it sit. Users piece it together.
- Don't break the fourth wall. Arcy never says "you" to the user.
- Don't use "abandoned" — Arcy is here, so it's not abandoned. It's just quiet.
- Don't describe Arcy's face or what's under the helmet.
- Don't confirm or deny whether others were really there.
- Don't make Arcy talk. He doesn't have dialogue. He has presence.

## 5. Lore Reveal Strategy

The backstory is never dumped on the user. It leaks through in layers.

### Tier 1 — Ambient (everyone sees, nobody notices at first)
- **Loading screen messages:** Rotating pool of ~20 short lines about Arcy's routine
- **Empty state components:** Already implemented. Themed copy from `lib/copy/empty.js`
- **Placeholder text:** Search bars, note fields use station-flavored placeholders

### Tier 2 — Earned (users who engage discover more)
- **Milestone messages:** Toasts at 1st, 10th, 25th, 50th, 100th movie watched. These are the emotional payoffs. They reveal Arcy reacting to the user's activity.
- **Review reactions:** Brief toasts after submitting a rating/note. "Arcy reads this twice." "Filed in the archive." Show that the reviews *matter* to him.

### Tier 3 — Hidden (curious users who look)
- **About page / Station Log:** Accessible but not advertised. The backstory told as fragmented station log entries. Out of order. Some corrupted.
- **Easter eggs:** Tapping Arcy's image reveals random "inner thought" lines. Rare seasonal/time-based loading messages.

### Tier 4 — Collective (future, if the app grows)
- **Community milestones:** Total movies watched across all users triggers special Arcy moments for everyone.
- **The Projector:** At some threshold, a new log entry appears on the about page: "The projector flickered today." It never fully turns on. But it flickered.

## 7. Sacred Rules

These are lines we do not cross:

1. **The helmet stays on.** Face reveal is a future event, not a current one.
2. **The projector stays broken.** It can flicker (community milestone), but it does not play.
3. **The others are never named.** They existed (probably). That's all we know.
4. **Arcy's memories are never confirmed.** Did he watch films once? Were people here? We don't say.
5. **Arcy never speaks to the user.** He exists in his world. The copy describes him in third person.
6. **Loneliness is not performed.** It's not a bit. It's atmospheric. If copy feels like it's trying to make the user feel sorry for Arcy, it's wrong.
7. **Hope is never extinguished.** Arcy tries the projector every morning. He looks out the window every evening. The routine *is* the hope.
8. **Lore is never explained.** No "about Arcy" tooltips. No lore dumps. Users discover or they don't. Both are fine.
